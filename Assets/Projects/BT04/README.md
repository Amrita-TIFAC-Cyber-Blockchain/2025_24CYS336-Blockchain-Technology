### Problem Statement
Traditional notary services rely on centralized authorities, physical documentation, and manual verification, often making the process slow, costly, and prone to tampering. As digital interactions grow, these systems struggle to meet the demand for faster, secure, and transparent document validation. While blockchain technology offers features like immutability, timestamping, and decentralized trust, it is yet to be fully adapted to replace core notarial functions. This research explores how a blockchain-based digital notary system can ensure document authenticity and integrity, while also addressing legal, privacy, and usability challenges identified in current notarial and technological frameworks.

-----
### Literature Survey 
1. **Blockchain in Digital Notary Services – Alina Goncharova (2024)** <br/>
This paper discusses how blockchain can modernize notary services by ensuring secure, tamper-proof documentation through immutability, timestamping, and decentralization. It emphasizes how such systems have already seen success in countries like Ukraine and across the EU, where paper-heavy processes were replaced with more transparent digital ones.
A key insight from this work is that while blockchain handles document validation efficiently, it still cannot verify human intent—a limitation where traditional notaries play a crucial role. The paper urges the need for legal reforms and notary adaptation to digital systems. This helped frame the legal relevance and practical justification for using blockchain in my project.

2. **A Disruptive Blockchain Framework for Notary Smart Contract and Digital Record Keeping – Nasr et al. (2023)** <br/>
This paper presents a working technical framework using Ethereum smart contracts to automate the notary process. It introduces a Decentralized Application (DApp) where users can upload documents, generate cryptographic hashes, and verify authenticity later—all without relying on central authorities.
The clarity of the system’s workflow and architecture offered direct inspiration for how my project could be designed technically. It contributed significantly to shaping my understanding of the components needed for a blockchain-powered notary system, especially smart contract logic and user interactions.

3. **Digital Trust Redefined: Blockchain-Based Notarization System Using eID Card – Deepika et al. (2025)** <br/>
This paper focuses on combining national digital identity cards (eID) with blockchain-based notarization. It shows how eID systems can authenticate users securely and legally, and how blockchain ensures that documents cannot be altered once uploaded.
What stood out was the emphasis on user identity verification—something blockchain alone cannot handle. This insight added value to my project's direction by highlighting how trust and identity can be built into decentralized systems, especially in legally sensitive applications like notarization.
