### Problem Statement

The real estate sector continues to face significant inefficiencies and trust-related challenges due to the shortcomings of traditional centralized systems. Issues such as title forgery, illegal ownership transfers, lack of tamper-proof and unified property records, dependency on costly intermediaries, and inadequate fraud detection mechanisms undermine transparency and security in property transactions. Existing frameworks fail to provide verifiable and immutable records, resulting in distrust among buyers, sellers, and regulatory authorities.Thus, there is a pressing need to develop a secure, transparent, and decentralized framework for real estate transactions that leverages blockchain technology to overcome the limitations of traditional systems.

### Literature Survey 

From Paper 1 (IEEE Paper):
Developing a Blockchain-Based Decentralized Application for Real Estate Transactions
•	Recent studies emphasize that smart contracts can automate ownership transfer, reducing intermediaries and transaction delays.
•	Blockchain improves transparency in property transactions by providing tamper-proof, immutable records of ownership and transaction history.
•	Real-world pilot applications have shown reduced transaction time, cost efficiency, and increased user trust by eliminating paperwork and manual verification.
•	The research highlights the importance of front-end and back-end integration with blockchain to build end-to-end decentralized property management systems.

From Paper 2 (ResearchGate Paper):
A Comprehensive Data Analytics Study on the Use of Blockchain Technology in Real Estate
•	Data analytics combined with blockchain provides real-time fraud detection, utilizing machine learning to monitor suspicious transactions.
•	Integration of geospatial analytics with blockchain enhances property validation by mapping property data and identifying illegal constructions or disputes.
•	The study suggests that predictive analytics on transaction history can provide risk scores for properties, helping buyers and regulators make informed decisions.
•	The paper identifies a gap in business intelligence solutions in real estate, recommending fraud detection dashboards and compliance monitoring tools.
